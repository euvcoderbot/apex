How to read these measurements
Qualifying pace
One fastest valid lap per team across Q1, Q2 and Q3, from either teammate. The deficit is (team lap time ÷ fastest team lap time − 1) × 100. No phase averaging. Official classification time must match an eligible dry timing lap. The season view averages event-relative deficits and sets its best average to zero.
Race pace
Dry, accurate green-flag laps, excluding pit laps and the opening two laps. A two-second timing-line gap filter runs in the background; it cannot establish clean air throughout a lap. A robust model estimates each driver while adjusting for shared race-lap conditions, compound and tyre age. Drivers need at least ten eligible laps, and comparisons must be identifiable from the available data. The faster eligible teammate represents the team. Fuel load, management and strategy cannot be fully separated; low-coverage races remain uncertain.
Cornering
One validated qualifying lap per team is lined up by GPS. The same turns are measured for every team in that event. The bands are based on typical apex speed: slow up to 120 km/h, medium up to 200, fast above 200. A +0.20% corner gap means about 0.18 seconds across those corners on a 90-second lap. Events with only some valid teams still contribute, but team circuit counts can differ.
Straights and acceleration
The main straight score estimates time from native speed over the same supported distance windows. It is not measured traversal time or isolated engine power. It includes setup, tow and deployment choices. Acceleration bands separately measure time to gain the selected speed range; they do not add up to lap time. Unsupported ranges remain unavailable.
Braking
Time through approach shows extra seconds to cross the same straight section leading into a corner. It reflects entry speed, braking point and deceleration. Same-speed slowing shows extra seconds to shed the same speed range. For example, +0.102 s means about a tenth of a second longer per measured braking zone, not over the whole lap. Both approaches use the same fastest valid qualifying lap, matched zones and supported entrants. The team view uses its fastest teammate; the driver view uses each driver's own fastest lap. Public telemetry provides brake on/off, not brake pressure or friction-brake power. GPS turn-in and sparse car-data samples limit precision.
Pit stops
Stationary time is the car stopped at its pit box; pit-lane time runs from pit entry to exit, including the stop. Neither is the race-time loss against a car that stays on track. Averages use measured visits for each team or driver; long stops and penalties are included. OpenF1 provides pit-lane timing from 2023 and stationary timing only from the 2024 US Grand Prix onward. Earlier pit-lane times are paired FastF1 entry and exit timestamps. Missing stationary times are never estimated.
Tyre trend
For 2021 onward, tyre-age change groups each driver's usable stints by the exact Pirelli dry compound (C1–C5, plus C6 in 2025), mapped from the race's Hard/Medium/Soft allocation. Unverified years or rounds are excluded rather than guessed. A grade's result averages its race estimates; overall first averages observed grades within each race, then averages races. C grades are comparable within a season, not necessarily between seasons. The fuel correction is estimated, so this is not pure tyre wear. The separate matched-rivals view compares stints on overlapping race laps, the same weekend compound and similar tyre age; it needs rival support. Check the event, stint and lap counts before drawing season-wide conclusions.
Season and progress
Each valid event can contribute even when another team lacks GPS. Missing races are not filled in, so teams may have different circuit counts. The progress table compares each team’s qualifying gap in its opening and latest three observed races; a more negative change means it closed the gap. That is relative one-lap progress, not proof an upgrade made the car faster.
Downforce
Not estimated. Public telemetry lacks the tyre, ride-height, wind, mass and aero-load inputs needed for a defensible downforce index. High-speed corner performance is shown directly instead.
Reliability and results
Official finishing status separates known mechanical failures, incidents and other/unclassified retirements. Points and average finishing position describe results, not car speed. Unclassified causes are never assumed mechanical.

Sources: FastF1 timing and telemetry · FIA event documents. Completed source data and race-corner snapshots are cached across visits. Calculation changes use a new cache version; recent weekends revalidate quickly for late corrections. Refresh data replaces cached results for your selected events.
