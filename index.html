How to read these measurements
Qualifying pace
One fastest valid lap per team across Q1, Q2 and Q3, from either teammate. The deficit is (team lap time ÷ fastest team lap time − 1) × 100. No phase averaging. Official classification time must match an eligible dry timing lap. The season view averages event-relative deficits and sets its best average to zero.
Race pace
Dry, accurate green-flag laps, excluding pit laps and the opening two laps. A two-second timing-line gap filter runs in the background; it cannot establish clean air throughout a lap. A robust model estimates each driver while adjusting for shared race-lap conditions, compound and tyre age. Drivers need at least ten eligible laps, and comparisons must be identifiable from the available data. The faster eligible teammate represents the team. Fuel load, management and strategy cannot be fully separated; low-coverage races remain uncertain.
Cornering
One real qualifying lap per team, with up to three candidates within 1% of its best. All cars are aligned by GPS to a common distance grid. Braking, apex and exit define identical windows; flat-out bends count as straights. Field-median apex speed sets the bands: low ≤120, medium ≤200, high >200 km/h. Each band sums all its corner times. Its time loss against the reference lap is divided by the reference full lap time. Losing 0.18 seconds across high-speed corners on a 90-second lap means +0.20%. Frozen speed and incomplete laps are excluded; at least 70% of the selected field must pass.
Straights and speed-domain acceleration
Straight performance evaluates speed-matched acceleration across fixed velocity bands (200→250, 250→300, 300→320 km/h) sampled from continuous full-throttle intervals in clean air (>3.0s gap). The primary headline metric is 250→300 km/h relative delta against the field median rebased to 0.000s, eliminating the direct initial speed advantage inherited from corner exit. Terminal speed is measured within an 80m shared corridor ending 10m before the field braking onset on straights ≥400m. Straight Traversal Delta (% of lap) is retained strictly for lap-time attribution and not as a straight-line performance ranking.
Peak speed, P95 and braking
Peak is the highest speed sample. A P95 of 320 km/h means 95% of full-throttle samples were at or below 320 km/h, and 5% were faster. Braking compares matched zones using distance, duration, distance-normalized deceleration (a_norm) and elapsed time delta (Δt). Telemetry discretization distance is Δs ~ v_entry / 3.7 Hz, and discrete onset points are shown as telemetry discretization brackets [last non-brake, first brake] m rather than artificial symmetric error bounds. The public telemetry brake channel is on/off and does not provide brake pressure.
Tyre trend
Robust lap-time slope within each eligible dry stint, with at least six laps and a five-lap age span. Stints are combined by event and compound, then events equally averaged. Overall is the equal average of soft, medium and hard trends and requires all three. Fuel, track evolution and management remain included.
Season and development
Corner and straight rankings use a common set of circuits for all ranked teams. Teams need telemetry coverage at 70% of loaded events to enter that comparison. Event-relative lap-share losses receive equal circuit weight; the team with the best mean total lap gap is the season reference at zero. The same reference adjustment is applied to every section, preserving the total. Driver execution, weather and track mix remain part of the observation.
Downforce
Not estimated. Public telemetry lacks the tyre, ride-height, wind, mass and aero-load inputs needed for a defensible downforce index. High-speed corner performance is shown directly instead.
Reliability and results
Official finishing status separates known mechanical failures, incidents and other/unclassified retirements. Points and average finishing position describe results, not car speed. Unclassified causes are never assumed mechanical.

Sources: FastF1 timing and telemetry · FIA event documents. Data is fetched on demand; no performance results are saved between visits.
