How to read these measurements
Qualifying pace
One fastest valid lap per team across Q1, Q2 and Q3, from either teammate. The deficit is (team lap time ÷ fastest team lap time − 1) × 100. No phase averaging. Official classification time must match an eligible dry timing lap. The season view averages event-relative deficits and sets its best average to zero.
Race pace
Dry, accurate green-flag laps, excluding pit laps and the opening two laps. A two-second timing-line gap filter runs in the background; it cannot establish clean air throughout a lap. A robust model estimates each driver while adjusting for shared race-lap conditions, compound and tyre age. Drivers need at least ten eligible laps, and comparisons must be identifiable from the available data. The faster eligible teammate represents the team. Fuel load, management and strategy cannot be fully separated; low-coverage races remain uncertain.
Cornering
One validated qualifying lap per team is lined up by GPS. The same turns are measured for every team in that event. The bands are based on typical apex speed: slow up to 120 km/h, medium up to 200, fast above 200. A +0.20% corner gap means about 0.18 seconds across those corners on a 90-second lap. Events with only some valid teams still contribute, but team circuit counts can differ.
Straights and acceleration
Straight traversal is observed lap time on non-corner sections. It includes speed carried out of corners and, in 2026, energy deployment. The selectable 50 km/h acceleration bands measure how long a car takes to gain that speed on a comparable full-throttle straight; 50–100 km/h may often have no qualifying support. A band appears only with enough comparable traces. Band times are separate diagnostics and do not add up to lap time. Higher bands may be unavailable where cars never reach them.
Braking
The headline uses only qualifying laps and compares time through identical straight-line braking windows, ending at GPS-detected turn-in. A +0.700% reading is about 0.525 seconds on a 75-second lap. It still reflects entry speed and driver technique, not just brakes. Energy-loss rate (kW/t) is calculated from speed over the first 50 m of braking; drag and energy recovery also contribute. Public telemetry does not provide brake pressure or friction-brake power, so this is not the AWS kW figure.
Tyre trend
The chart compares how lap time changes as a tyre ages versus rival stints on overlapping race laps, the same compound and similar tyre age. It needs at least two rival teams and eight supported laps. This reduces shared fuel and track effects, but does not isolate tyre wear from driver management, traffic or tyre history. Soft, medium and hard can be viewed separately; overall needs at least two supported compounds and gives each equal weight. Check the number of supported events before reading the rank as a season-wide conclusion.
Season and progress
Each valid event can contribute even when another team lacks GPS. Missing races are not filled in, so teams may have different circuit counts. The progress table compares each team’s qualifying gap in its opening and latest three observed races; a more negative change means it closed the gap. That is relative one-lap progress, not proof an upgrade made the car faster.
Downforce
Not estimated. Public telemetry lacks the tyre, ride-height, wind, mass and aero-load inputs needed for a defensible downforce index. High-speed corner performance is shown directly instead.
Reliability and results
Official finishing status separates known mechanical failures, incidents and other/unclassified retirements. Points and average finishing position describe results, not car speed. Unclassified causes are never assumed mechanical.

Sources: FastF1 timing and telemetry · FIA event documents. Data is fetched on demand; no performance results are saved between visits.
